# Ian Helfrich — Living Structure

## Product context

This is the public website of Dr. Ian Helfrich, an applied economist, quantitative research designer, educator, and systems builder. It must let a sophisticated reader see intellectual range and evidentiary care quickly, while remaining intelligible to an executive, student, or collaborator who does not speak academic jargon.

Primary architecture: Home, Work, Research, Teaching & Coaching, About, CV, Contact. Supporting records remain available through Projects, Datasets, Library, Writing, and Search. The homepage is not a résumé pasted into cards. It is a coherent introduction with selected evidence and one or two genuinely useful interactive instruments.

## Non-negotiable content boundaries

- NMTC is one piece of work, not the singular flagship or definition of Ian's career.
- Research must be grouped and represented according to its verified maturity and authorship.
- Ian is the sole author and originator of the NMTC manuscript; Katia Antunes and Elizaveta Gonchar are contributors, not coauthors.
- Do not invent papers, results, titles, dates, affiliations, metrics, or claims.
- Teaching and tutoring must be described through durable capabilities, not named learner-specific builds.
- Use the user-confirmed record for the separate `1,035+` Wyzant hours and over `1,000` private hours.
- Include confirmed Journal of Economic Theory referee service only within professional service.
- Never use `public/people/ian.webp`, the formal pink-shirt headshot.
- Use `public/people/ian-editorial.webp` as the durable portrait. Preserve its square composition, full face and hand, tonal character, and uncropped intimacy.
- Elizaveta is an emotional design influence: warmth, relational intelligence, exacting collaboration. Do not expose private relationship detail as public copy or decoration.

## Creative premise: Living Structure

The design should feel constructed, alive, and intellectually exact. Its originality comes from joining four logics rather than copying four aesthetics:

1. **Gaudí / Barcelona:** load-bearing curves, catenary geometry, mosaic as assembled evidence, irregularity that resolves into structure. Never use tourist motifs, faux mosaics, stained-glass clichés, or ornamental organic blobs.
2. **UNC / Carolina:** breathable space, open sky, optimism, legibility, generosity. This is spatial and atmospheric, not collegiate branding.
3. **Ukraine:** graphic clarity, courage, blue and yellow as concentrated signals, and occasional red as human urgency. Never render a literal flag treatment.
4. **Teenage Engineering:** tactile precision, exposed controls, small labels, instrument logic, delightful but disciplined interaction. Never turn the site into a dashboard or gadget museum.

The resulting visual language is a continuous spatial composition, not a stack of cards. Curves, rules, and color fragments should carry meaning: links between questions and methods; transitions between uncertainty and usable form; evidence assembled into decisions.

## Palette

No cream, navy, copper, teal, dusty pink, gray-on-gray fieldbook palette, or generic purple gradient.

- **Porcelain** `#FCFCFB`: principal background. It must read as white, not cream.
- **Carbon** `#171512`: principal text and dark surfaces; warm black, never navy.
- **Carolina air** `#8EC5E8`: large atmospheric field and hover state.
- **Kyiv blue** `#0057B8`: primary signal and link color.
- **Sunflower** `#FFD43B`: highlight, focus, active state, and selected evidence.
- **Vermilion** `#E94B35`: sparse counter-signal for tension, warnings, and one compositional accent.
- **Ultraviolet** `#6546D7`: rare secondary analytic category, at most 5% of a screen.
- **Paper shadow** `rgba(23, 21, 18, 0.12)`: physical separation only.

At least 65% of the homepage should remain Porcelain/white space. Carbon carries typography. Saturated colors occur in deliberate fields or one-pixel/two-pixel structural lines, not confetti.

## Typography

Use typography with institutional credibility and mechanical clarity, without the current newspaper/editorial look.

- **Primary grotesk:** `Mona Sans Variable`, self-hosted, for navigation, body copy, headlines, buttons, and captions. It should feel intelligent, open, and contemporary.
- **Expressive display:** `Fraunces Variable`, self-hosted, used only for short human phrases, research questions, or a single italic counterpoint. Never use it for all headings or long body copy.
- **Instrument mono:** `Commit Mono`, self-hosted, for measurements, status, methods, keyboard hints, and control labels.

Hero headline: grotesk, 58–104px desktop, 42–58px tablet, 34–44px mobile; weight 560–650; line-height 0.92–1.00; letter-spacing -0.045em. Never split a word. Headline width should be shaped intentionally, not centered in a generic container.

Body: 18–21px desktop, 17–19px mobile; line-height 1.5–1.62; maximum 64ch. Labels: 11–12px mono, uppercase only when the label is operational. Avoid aphoristic fragments, false gravitas, slogan stacking, excessive em dashes, and “AI voice” parallelism.

## Geometry and layout

- Desktop uses a 16-column responsive grid with 20–28px gutters and a 96rem maximum, but visible content may break the grid through curves or full-bleed color fields.
- Mobile is single-column with 20px edge padding; interactive controls wrap, never horizontally scroll.
- No universal rounded cards. Information groups should be separated by alignment, rules, color fields, and whitespace.
- Radius is semantic: 0px for scholarly records; 4–8px for tactile controls; 999px only for an actual toggle/knob/continuous control; asymmetric 32–160px curves only for large structural fields.
- Use catenary/cubic Bézier arcs as recurring structural dividers. Arcs should connect a question to evidence or one domain to another; they are not background squiggles.
- Mosaic fragments are rectilinear or gently curved colored panels containing real evidence snippets, methods, or links. Each fragment must remain readable and clickable.

## Homepage composition

### Header

Thin, transparent-to-solid sticky header. Ian Helfrich set plainly at left; primary routes across the center/right; compact square Contact control. No pills. On mobile, a tactile two-state menu control reveals a full-height sheet with large route labels and one visible close action.

### Hero

Use the square editorial portrait at substantial scale, never cropped or desaturated further. The portrait is one of three structural anchors with the headline and a living catenary/mosaic instrument.

Required copy hierarchy:

- Identity: `Ian Helfrich, PhD`
- Plain statement: `Applied economist, quantitative research designer, and educator.`
- Human claim, natural and bounded: one short sentence explaining that Ian turns consequential but poorly framed problems into models, tools, and explanations people can use.
- Two actions: `See the work` and `Discuss a problem`.

The hero must demonstrate range without listing every domain. Audience controls re-rank actual evidence records and open their Question / Role / Artifact / Limit fields. Motion must correspond to that change in order; it cannot imply a quantity that does not exist.

### Selected work

Three to five records arranged as a connected field, not equal cards. Each record shows: question, status, Ian's role, method, what exists, and limit. Balance research, policy/consulting, teaching/coaching, and systems work. NMTC can appear, but never receive disproportionate visual dominance.

### Working range

A spatial continuum connects research, consulting, teaching/coaching, and system building. Use real examples such as NNCTA, KSA/consulting where verified, university teaching, quantitative research design coaching, and supported research records. The interaction reveals evidence without naming private clients or learners.

### Closing

One clear invitation, contact routes, and compact factual footer. Do not end with a motivational slogan.

## Interaction and motion

- Preserve and visually integrate the site's real research instruments, especially least-squares/regression, optimal transport, representation relay, and research graph where they substantively fit.
- New signature interaction: an audience-routed evidence browser. Selecting a reader type re-ranks real records, changes the selected evidence, and preserves direct artifact URLs.
- Controls should feel physical: 1–2px outlines, decisive down states, tiny numeric/semantic readouts, and no glossy skeuomorphism.
- Scroll reveals are short and local: 180–420ms. Avoid whole-page fade-up choreography.
- Use View Transitions only for continuity between related records.
- Pointer parallax maximum 6px. No cursor hijacking, scroll-jacking, looping ambient motion, or animation that competes with reading.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; every instrument remains fully usable with keyboard, touch, and motion disabled.

## Signature system: evidence that does work

The homepage is an evidence browser and workbench, not a decorative visualization. Its distinctiveness comes from letting visitors use Ian's reasoning patterns immediately.

- The audience control (`Research collaborator`, `Organization`, `Learner`, `Hiring team`) re-ranks the same verified record set. The resulting motion is an animated sort and carries only ordinal navigation meaning.
- Each record uses Question / Role / Artifact / Limit as its stable schema. Selecting a record opens those fields in place and provides a real destination to the underlying paper, report, public profile, project, or evidence ledger.
- The portrait remains a large intact square and the human anchor of the first screen. It is never a graph node, badge, avatar, or draggable object.
- The first public instrument is an estimate-to-decision relay. Effect, standard error, threshold, interval geometry, verdict, and copyable prose are mechanically linked.
- The second public instrument is a browser-only research-design triage. It compiles the decision, current evidence state, primary threat, and operational consequence into a copyable scoping brief, while explicitly refusing to choose an estimator or claim identification.
- Additional tools should surface existing, verified components: the reasoning chain, confounding lab, specification workbench, and Applied Statistics system. Do not create a museum of disconnected demos.
- No chart-shaped object may use arbitrary geometry. Any movement, mark, scale, or order must correspond to an actual input, calculation, category, or navigation priority that can be explained in one sentence.
- On small screens, audience controls wrap, evidence records stack, forms remain single-column, and every interaction must coexist with normal page scrolling.

## Portrait treatment

The editorial portrait is visually intact in a square. It may sit inside an architectural frame formed by a one-pixel catenary arc, a solid Carolina-air field, or two offset color planes. Do not mask the face into a circle, arch, blob, or irregular crop. Do not place text over the eyes or hand. Do not apply color duotone, additional sepia, blur, or low-opacity treatment.

## Component language

- Links: Carbon text with a Kyiv-blue structural underline that grows from the relevant edge.
- Primary actions: square/rectangular Carbon or Kyiv-blue button, 46–52px high, 4px radius, visible 2px focus state in Sunflower.
- Secondary actions: text link with arrow or coordinate marker, not an outlined pill.
- Status: compact mono line with color dash; no badge soup.
- Evidence record: open layout with question, role/method rail, result/existence block, limit, and inspectable artifact link.
- Instrument frame: thin Carbon rule, one colored calibration edge, transparent/white field, controls grouped like a compact device panel.

## Voice

Write like a rigorous, self-aware scholar and useful collaborator. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Explain jargon at the point of use without condescension. Let case structure demonstrate judgment instead of claiming “systems thinking,” “translation,” “clarity,” or “rigor.” Avoid generic portfolio language such as “explore my journey,” “impactful solutions,” “where ideas meet action,” or “turning complexity into clarity.”

## Accessibility and performance

- WCAG AA minimum; aim for AAA in body copy.
- All interactive targets at least 44×44 CSS pixels on touch.
- Visible keyboard focus in Sunflower against both white and Carbon.
- Semantic HTML, one H1, logical heading order, descriptive links, useful image alt text.
- No horizontal overflow at 320px.
- Local fonts with `font-display: swap`; subset sensibly.
- Motion must not obscure state; live regions announce completed computational changes only.
- Keep the static shell fast. Interactive instruments hydrate locally and only when present.

## Explicit exclusions

- No pink-shirt portrait.
- No cream/navy/copper/teal palette.
- No fieldbook, broadsheet, dossier, notebook, laboratory-dashboard, or generic SaaS aesthetic.
- No card wall, pill-button grammar, pastel blobs, glassmorphism, faux 3D chrome, or gratuitous gradients.
- No literal Gaudí mosaic background, Spanish tile texture, Ukrainian flag banner, UNC logo treatment, or Teenage Engineering imitation.
- No invented decorative logo or monogram.
