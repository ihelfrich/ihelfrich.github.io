# Public prose

Dr. Helfrich rejected the site's generic promotional and tutorial narration in September 2026. Apply this editorial direction to pages, content collections, interactive controls, and generated explanations.

- Write specific statements about the subject. Delete sentences that only announce the next section, describe the interface, or congratulate the reader.
- Use descriptive headings. Avoid slogans, sentence fragments in pairs or triplets, and formulas such as “Your move, explained” or “From X to Y.”
- Give instructions only when the interaction needs them. A labeled control usually needs no accompanying paragraph.
- Explain mathematical approximations in the calculation where they matter. State research limitations and data provenance precisely; do not attach generic disclaimers to every card or link.
- Keep concrete examples and approachable explanations. Removing filler must not turn introductory lessons into unexplained notation.
- Use connected paragraphs for prose. Reserve numbered steps, bold labels, cards, and callout boxes for actual sequences, comparisons, or controls.
- Do not invent personality, anecdotes, credentials, motivation, or praise for Dr. Helfrich. Preserve attribution, evidence dates, numerical results, and research status.
- If an archived post is substantially edited, identify it as revised and retain its original publication date.
- Review the rendered page as well as the source. Empty labels, repeated summaries, and excessive visual compartments are editorial problems too.
