# History of Economic Thought — draft review

Status: 60 original draft items, six units of ten. No runtime activation. All items use constructed scenarios or original interpretive questions; hypothetical quantities are not historical estimates.

## Scope and prerequisites

Original exercises about economic arguments and the assumptions that distinguish them, rather than name-and-date trivia or a chronology of economic events. Prerequisites: introductory microeconomics and macroeconomics, algebra and ratios; basic differentiation supports marginal-utility examples. Conditional expectations and regression concepts help with modern-methodology extensions. Named attributions are tied to primary texts; constructed numerical applications and modern formal reconstructions are labeled as such. The selection covers parts of the classical, marginalist, Marxian, institutional, Keynesian and modern methodological traditions without claiming exhaustive global coverage or a single uncontested account of disciplinary progress.

## Primary-text attribution checks

- Smith: Wealth of Nations I.i, I.iii and I.vii support division of labor, market extent and natural/market price distinctions.
- Ricardo: the preface, rent chapter, foreign-trade chapter and machinery chapter support the distributional focus, differential rent, comparative costs and qualified machinery argument.
- Jevons: Theory of Political Economy chapters on utility and exchange support final utility and the distinction between past labor and current value. Later ordinal representations are explicitly labeled as later constructions.
- Marshall: Principles preface supports provisional isolation of forces and adjustment-dependent time horizons.
- Marx: Capital volume I chapters 1, 6 and 9 support socially necessary labor, labor-power and surplus/necessary labor categories. Numerical rate comparisons are conditional illustrations.
- Veblen: Theory of the Leisure Class supports pecuniary emulation and conspicuous consumption; items do not attribute a single motive to all consumption.
- Keynes: General Theory chapters 3, 7, 11–13 and 19 support effective demand, saving/investment accounting, expected capital returns, confidence, liquidity preference and multiple wage-adjustment channels. Textbook models are identified as interpretations.
- Hayek: The Use of Knowledge in Society supports time-and-place knowledge and the tin-price illustration. No universal efficiency claim is inferred.
- Friedman: The Methodology of Positive Economics supports evaluating implications in their intended domain; the questions do not claim all assumptions are irrelevant.

The precise primary-text URLs appear in each item’s sourceUrls. Named historical attributions were checked against accessible primary passages. Modern constructed methodology examples are not presented as verbatim historical propositions.
