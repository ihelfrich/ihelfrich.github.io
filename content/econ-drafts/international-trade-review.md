## Clarifications incorporated during authoring

- Strict gains use an interior trading price; a boundary price is not called a strict gain for both countries.
- The Ricardian boundary-price question permits home diversification instead of claiming forced full specialization.
- Specific-factor real wages are expressed separately in both goods; overall welfare is not asserted without preferences. The separate Cobb-Douglas example supplies those preferences and uses the exact price index.
- Heckscher–Ohlin comparative statics require diversification, fixed goods prices where relevant, and a stable factor-intensity ranking. Specialization is explicitly distinguished from factor-price equalization.
- Fixed export costs are separated from operating profit; aggregate productivity reallocation is separated from within-firm improvement.
- Quota rents are assigned to their stated owner. Tariff losses exclude domestic revenue transfers; large-country terms-of-trade gains are not assumed for small countries.
- Gravity coefficient arithmetic uses the exact multiplicative expression; it is not confused with the local percentage approximation. Empirical association is not presented as causation.
