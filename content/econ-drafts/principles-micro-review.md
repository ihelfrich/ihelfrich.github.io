# Principles of Microeconomics draft review

Author-reviewed standalone undergraduate introduction: 60 original constructed questions in six units of ten. The course is non-calculus and uses the title Principles of Microeconomics, not an AP label. Existing AP and intermediate micro content is not relabeled or incorporated. Only the three assigned draft files were written in the repository; no runtime, activation, log or commit changes.

## Verified curriculum sources

Verified October 13, 2026:

- MIT 14.01, Principles of Microeconomics, Fall 2023 calendar: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2023/pages/calendar/. The official progression covers markets, preferences and budgets, production and costs, competition, welfare and applications. MIT uses some calculus; this course borrows coverage breadth, not its mathematical level.
- UC Berkeley Economics 1, Fall 2019 syllabus: https://eml.berkeley.edu/~olney/fall19/econ1/syllabus.pdf. Verified its introductory micro sequence: opportunity cost and trade, supply/demand, price mechanisms, surplus and taxation, elasticity, firms, market power, information, externalities and labor. The combined course also includes macroeconomics; this draft uses only its micro coverage.

These are official sources from two R1 institutions. They guide topic selection, not question text or numerical scenarios. No questions were copied from assessments. The local principles curriculum entry explicitly distinguishes undergraduate principles from AP and notes that calculus intensity varies; this draft deliberately offers a non-calculus route. All data and settings are hypothetical.

## Distinctness and level

Inspected AP generated families in `bank.mjs`, AP concepts in `concepts.mjs`, intermediate numeric families in `other-courses.mjs`, and intermediate concepts in `undergrad-concepts.mjs`. Avoided derivative-based choice, Cobb–Douglas exercises, Slutsky decomposition, continuous Cournot calculations and proof-level welfare. The new course uses project-set enumeration, nonlinear production tables, resale decisions, demand/supply schedules, utility tables, finite production options, contractual information and policy evidence. Core concepts necessarily overlap an introductory curriculum, but these are newly composed tasks rather than renamed or numerically varied existing questions. Exact prompt comparison against the four source files found no duplicates; this is not by itself a semantic originality proof, so manual review also considered task structure.

All tested skills require only arithmetic, percentages, elementary algebra, probability and table reading. Optional next-course extensions appear separately in the outline and are not prerequisites for the 60 items.

## Executed checks and repairs

Independently enumerated every project subset under the ten-token budget: A+C uniquely gives the highest feasible benefit, 18. Enumerated all utility bundles costing at most six: two coffees and one snack uniquely produce utility 21. Evaluated all competitive outputs, giving profits −20,−19,−15,−14,−18; evaluated all monopoly outputs, giving 0,9,14,15,12. Enumerated both players’ advertising choices: Advertise/Advertise is the sole pure Nash equilibrium. Checked every efficient-trade count: total surplus is 0,15,21,18 for zero through three trades, so two is optimal.

Exact-fraction arithmetic verified all other numerical keys: production costs 10/20/30, comparative costs 1/4 and 1/2, attendance 25 versus resale 30, extension benefit 25, shortage 40, inventory 25, revenues 1,000 and 1,080, elasticities 1 and 3, tax burden 2/1, subsidy receipt 9, budget cost 24, third-book benefit 8, extra take-home pay 22, compensating job attribute at least 40, future consumption 110, lottery utility 5 versus certainty utility 8, deductible payout 8, short-run loss 14, sixth-unit cost 12, accounting/economic profit 35,000/10,000, fourth-worker output 4, hiring profit −4, technique costs 48/40 then 60/70, scale costs 8/6, marginal revenue 4, surplus split 8/7, blocked trade gain 6, externality surplus −2, shared project surplus 4, imports 55, tariff revenue 60 and reform net surplus 40.

Self-review clarified 4.10 by stating downward-sloping market demand explicitly, making the entry-to-price reasoning concrete. Parent review tightened 2.5 to include unchanged equilibrium quantity as a possibility, 3.6 to state that pay and schedule are the only differing job attributes, and 3.7 to measure future money rather than unspecified purchasing power. No arithmetic key required repair. Reviewed every option for alternative correctness and each inference for unnecessary certainty; in particular, revenue changes are not equated with profit changes, defaults are not presumed welfare-improving, market share alone does not establish market power, and selected subsidy outcomes do not identify a causal effect.

Final saved-file checks cover 60 unique IDs/topics, six ten-item units, exactly three full-string distractors and four distinct choices per item, nonempty prompt/explanation/hint/source fields, draft/constructed labels and exact outline-topic alignment. No rendered-page check or runtime activation was part of this content-only task.
