## Authoring safeguards and limitations

The official Yale field-description URL is curriculum provenance, not evidence for a real empirical estimate or a source of copied assessment questions. Its public-finance description anchors taxation, public goods, social insurance, social security, and policy methods. All scenarios and numbers are constructed; there are no asserted real program effects. Public-economics coverage follows the parent research map and is introductory/intermediate rather than a graduate field survey.

During authoring, clarified that the administrative expense in the weighted-transfer question is already embodied in the delivery shortfall, preventing double counting. Explicitly imposed full capitalization in the land-sale question and simultaneous marginal phaseouts in the tax-wedge question. Specified the welfare criterion for allocation comparisons and excluded financing distortions where using summed dollar benefits.

Remaining gate: independent economics review, rendering/accessibility checks, and activation by the parent. These drafts are 30 substantive authored questions, not 5,000 active items or evidence of comprehensive field coverage. Skill labels are author descriptions, not empirical difficulty calibration. Standard partial-equilibrium tax triangles use the stipulated linear supply/demand surplus framework; more general income effects and general-equilibrium interactions are outside those questions.
