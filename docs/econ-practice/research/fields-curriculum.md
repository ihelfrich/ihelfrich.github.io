# Economics fields curriculum map

Research date: 2026-10-13. This is an authoring blueprint, not a claim of active question coverage or exhaustive R1 coverage. Official catalogs establish course breadth; dated syllabi establish specific taught content. University names belong in provenance, not the student course picker. All proposed exercises must be original rather than copied from source assessments.

## Verified primary-source anchors

- **MIT Econometric Data Science, Spring 2022:** randomized trials, regression, IV, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity and simultaneous equations; theoretical and empirical work. [Syllabus](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Syl32SP22r3.pdf).
- **MIT Data Analysis for Social Scientists, Spring 2023:** probability/statistics, experimental design, regression, machine learning and visualization; graduate version has additional assignments. [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-310x-data-analysis-for-social-scientists-spring-2023/pages/syllabus/).
- **MIT International Trade, undergraduate, Fall 2016:** exchange and welfare, terms of trade, Ricardian/specific-factors/Heckscher–Ohlin models, inequality, increasing returns, tariffs, migration, multinationals and offshoring. [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-54-international-trade-fall-2016/pages/calendar/).
- **MIT Industrial Organization I, graduate, Fall 2022:** durable goods, discrimination, differentiated competition, demand estimation, search, dynamic competition, entry, investment, auctions, advertising and online markets. [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-271-industrial-organization-i-fall-2022/pages/calendar/).
- **MIT Development Economics, graduate, Fall 2021:** poverty traps, behavioral development, education, saving, health, family/gender, land, public finance, labor and credit. [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-771-development-economics-fall-2021/pages/calendar/).
- **MIT Labor Economics and Public Policy, Fall 2021:** combines labor theory and econometric evidence. [Syllabus](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/1464_Fall2021_combined_0.pdf).
- **Yale undergraduate catalog, 2026–27:** econometrics includes simultaneous equations, panels and limited outcomes. Financial economics includes portfolio choice, arbitrage, fixed income and derivatives; corporate finance adds investment and financing. Other named courses cover labor, education, development, health, climate/energy, competition policy, economic thought and European history. [Catalog](https://catalog.yale.edu/ycps/courses/econ/).
- **Yale graduate field descriptions:** advanced econometrics distinguishes asymptotics, nonparametrics, time series, panels and microeconometrics. Public finance combines public goods, insurance frictions, taxation, social security and reduced-form/structural methods. Development includes household/farm organization, networks and technology adoption; economic history has separate US and European sequences. [Fields](https://economics.yale.edu/phd-program/courses/field-descriptions).
- **Stanford course list, 2026–27:** separate undergraduate behavioral and experimental courses; graduate sequences in development, IO, econometrics, trade/spatial economics and behavioral/experimental economics; additional courses in matching, networks and computational economics. Listing establishes course existence, not each topic below. [Courses](https://economics.stanford.edu/courses).
- **Harvard concentrator guide:** behavioral economics studies departures from unlimited rationality and exclusively self-interested behavior, spanning multiple applied fields. [Guide](https://www.economics.harvard.edu/concentrator-guide).

## Proposed general course titles and authoring scope

These detailed topic allocations are a synthesis and authoring recommendation, not verbatim or exhaustive reconstructions of any one syllabus. Graduate extensions must be separately tagged and should not silently enter an undergraduate practice pool.

| General course title | Undergraduate modules to author | Advanced / graduate extensions | Distinct exercise families |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statistics for Economics | Conditional probability; Bayes; expectations and variance; sampling; LLN/CLT; confidence intervals; testing; power; multiple testing | Convergence concepts; delta method; empirical processes | Diagnose a sampling frame; interpret uncertainty; construct counterexamples to independence; compare power designs |
| Introductory Econometrics | OLS interpretation; logs/interactions; omitted variables; heteroskedasticity; robust inference; prediction versus causation | Matrix projections; asymptotic distributions; efficiency | Interpret coefficient tables; identify a bad control; repair units; explain why precision differs across samples |
| Causal Inference and Applied Econometrics | Potential outcomes; random assignment; noncompliance; IV/LATE; DiD; RD; fixed effects; clustering | Heterogeneous-treatment DiD; weak IV; partial identification; doubly robust estimation; sensitivity analysis | Choose an estimand; identify failed assumptions; read an event-study figure; distinguish selection from treatment effects |
| Advanced Econometrics | Likelihood; GMM; binary outcomes; panel models; forecasting; stationarity | Nonparametrics; bootstrap validity; cointegration; state-space models; dynamic panels; high-dimensional inference | Select valid moments; diagnose identification failure; compare forecast loss; interpret marginal effects |
| Labor Economics | Labor supply/demand; human capital; signaling; compensating differentials; discrimination; monopsony; minimum wages; unions; migration | Search/matching; wage posting; sorting; task models; equilibrium policy incidence | Compare competitive and monopsonistic responses; distinguish signaling from productivity; evaluate wage-gap claims |
| Public Economics | Incidence; excess burden; optimal commodity taxes; public goods; redistribution; social insurance; pensions; local public finance | Mirrlees taxation; sufficient statistics; dynamic public finance; mechanism design | Separate transfers from resource costs; compare insurance and incentive effects; trace tax wedges across markets |
| Industrial Organization | Cournot/Bertrand/Stackelberg; product differentiation; entry; collusion; discrimination; vertical restraints; regulation; platforms | Discrete-choice demand; structural supply; dynamic entry; auctions; search estimation | Diagnose strategic substitutes/complements; evaluate merger counterfactuals; distinguish concentration from market power |
| Development Economics | Poverty measurement; traps; credit/insurance failures; agriculture; education/health; migration; technology adoption; institutions | Household bargaining; networks; misallocation; structural transformation; program equilibrium effects | Contrast mechanisms behind low take-up; interpret a randomized intervention; detect spillovers; assess external validity |
| International Trade | Comparative advantage; specific factors; factor proportions; terms of trade; scale economies; tariffs/quotas; migration; offshoring | Gravity; heterogeneous firms; quantitative trade; global value chains; spatial equilibrium | Identify winners and losers; compare small/large-country tariffs; interpret gravity estimates; distinguish productivity from selection |
| Environmental and Resource Economics | Externalities; taxes/permits/standards; abatement; valuation; discounting; common resources; renewable/nonrenewable stocks; energy dispatch | Prices versus quantities under uncertainty; dynamic extraction; climate damages; integrated assessment; leakage | Choose instruments given uncertainty; distinguish stock/flow pollutants; compare discounting assumptions; interpret nonmarket valuation |
| Health Economics | Health demand; insurance; adverse selection; moral hazard; provider incentives; payment; cost-effectiveness | Risk adjustment; selection estimation; provider competition; dynamic health capital | Distinguish selection from utilization responses; interpret incremental cost-effectiveness; compare payment incentives |
| Behavioral and Experimental Economics | Present bias; loss aversion; reference dependence; probability weighting; social preferences; limited attention; experiment design | Identification of behavioral parameters; welfare with inconsistent preferences; belief elicitation; field/lab transport | Identify competing explanations; evaluate commitment choices; distinguish framing from payoff changes; diagnose experiment confounds |

| Economic History and History of Thought | Industrialization; institutions; migration; slavery and coercion; crises; technological change; classical/marginalist/Keynesian traditions | Historical measurement; archival selection; persistence designs; competing institutional mechanisms | Evaluate a historical source; compare explanations with explicit evidence; distinguish descriptive claims from normative commitments |

## Efficient expansion with pedagogical safeguards

1. Store one canonical concept identifier and prerequisite graph; expose it through multiple course pathways without counting the same item twice. Separate level, field, method and assessed skill.
2. Build family blueprints around distinct reasoning operations: derive, compare, diagnose, interpret evidence, identify assumptions, construct a counterexample, and recommend a policy conditional on an explicit objective. Changing names and coefficients does not create a new family.
3. For each module, author a small set of substantive conceptual/graph/table/model-comparison families before adding numeric variants. Parameter generators must preserve the intended economic regime and document boundary cases.
4. Mark constructed data as synthetic. A real policy or empirical finding requires its own verified source, year, population and uncertainty; a syllabus link alone does not verify the claim.
5. Use separate answer keys with reasoning and misconception-specific distractors. Include cases where the correct conclusion is underdetermination, but state exactly which assumption or evidence is missing.
6. Gate activation on content review, executable numeric checks where applicable, rendering and accessibility, and metadata completeness. Track author-seeded difficulty separately from empirical calibration. A 5,000-item display count must mean active items and separately disclose substantive families/variants.
7. Empirical identification and theory assumptions should be practiced in authentic settings, but normative disagreement is not an automatically scored misconception. Rubrics must specify the welfare criterion and maintained assumptions.

Remaining research: detailed current syllabi for environmental/resource, public, behavioral, health and political economy; non-US and less represented R1 programs; economic geography, agricultural, institutional/comparative systems and other specialized fields. The map is intentionally extensible and cannot support an “every model at every R1” claim.
