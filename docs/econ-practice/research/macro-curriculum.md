# Macroeconomics curriculum evidence and expansion map

Research pass: 2026-10-12. This is a curriculum design source file, not a claim of released item coverage. Official teaching materials were inspected from MIT, Columbia, and UC Berkeley. Archived offerings establish topics actually taught in those offerings; they do not establish current requirements or universal R1 coverage. Course names below are general navigation labels. Graduate material must remain visibly distinct from undergraduate material.

## Expansion design

The source matrix supports a prerequisite graph, not one long undifferentiated topic menu. Suggested structure: course → module → model/concept → observable skill. A model can appear at multiple levels, but each item should declare mathematical prerequisites, assumptions, solution method, and interpretation task. Example: a principles Solow item reads a capital diagram; intermediate asks a transition/steady-state calculation; graduate asks dynamic efficiency or optimality. Cross-list these carefully without counting the same item twice.

For each major model, author independent families spanning (1) assumptions and mechanisms, (2) calculations or equilibrium derivation, (3) graphs/dynamics, (4) comparative statics and policy counterfactuals, (5) data interpretation, and (6) failures or limits. Vary the economic reasoning, not just names and numbers. Macro item metadata should specify timing, real/nominal units, closure, expectation formation, policy regime, and whether a shock is temporary/permanent or anticipated/unanticipated. Those distinctions often determine the answer.

Avoid a single macro ability score across all these domains. Preserve separate course/module evidence; graduate computational or proof performance cannot be inferred from introductory multiple choice. New domains should use provisional routing until their content and psychometric behavior support stronger measurement claims.

## Gaps requiring further targeted evidence

This pass does not establish exhaustive coverage of heterogeneous-agent macro (Huggett/Aiyagari/Krusell–Smith/HANK), search and matching, modern monetary implementation, macroeconometrics, climate macro, demographic macro, or every contemporary field elective. They should be explicit research/authoring gaps, not silently marked covered. MIT's Spring 2025 [risk-centric macro-finance syllabus](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/macroFinanceRiskCentricSyllabus2025_v4.pdf) was verified as an official, recent extension source; detailed model extraction is still pending. Older graduate syllabi provide strong canonical foundations but require this contemporary supplementation.

No source problems or answer keys were copied. These sources establish curriculum scope and sequencing for original question authorship; they do not confer an endorsement or prove the platform's current questions cover these topics.
