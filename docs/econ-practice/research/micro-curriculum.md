# Microeconomics curriculum evidence and proposed taxonomy

Research date: 2026-10-13. Primary university sources only. This is a curriculum design input, not a claim that current bank items cover these topics. No university questions were copied. Source dates identify the actual course edition; older OCW offerings remain useful evidence of taught content, not evidence of current syllabi everywhere.

## Recommended implementation taxonomy (design recommendation)

Display general course titles: **Principles of Microeconomics; Intermediate Microeconomics; Advanced Microeconomics; Game Theory; Decision Theory and Behavioral Economics; Information and Contract Theory; Market Design**. Keep AP Micro as a separate exam-aligned route through overlapping introductory concepts. A course membership is a many-to-many mapping to concepts, not a duplicated item bank.

Within intermediate/advanced micro, organize reusable strands: choice/preferences; demand/duality; uncertainty/intertemporal choice; production/cost/duality; partial equilibrium; general equilibrium/welfare; strategic interaction; information/incentives. Store mathematical level independently: graphical/algebraic, single-variable calculus, multivariable optimization, proof-based. Separate empirical interpretation from theoretical derivation.

Recommended concept tags for authoring (these are our decomposition, not a claim every inspected syllabus names each one): completeness/transitivity/continuity/convexity; ordinal transformations; interior/corner solutions; Cobb–Douglas/CES/Leontief/quasilinear preferences; Marshallian/Hicksian demands; expenditure/indirect utility; Roy/Shephard/Slutsky identities; equivalent/compensating variation; WARP/GARP; technology sets/returns to scale; conditional factor demand; profit/cost duality; exchange economies/Edgeworth boxes; Walrasian equilibrium; Pareto efficiency/welfare theorems; market completeness; expected utility/certainty equivalents/risk premiums; first/second-order stochastic dominance; Nash/subgame-perfect/Bayesian equilibrium; incentive compatibility/participation constraints. Verify topic-specific authoritative derivations during item authorship.

Each concept should have distinct tasks: recognize assumptions; compute/derive; interpret a graph/table; compare policies; diagnose an incorrect argument; find a counterexample or boundary case. Numerical variants count separately from independently authored families. A 5,000-item target needs meaningful breadth and review evidence, not thousands of parameter substitutions within a few models.

## Limits and next validation

This bounded survey inspected MIT, Berkeley and Harvard official material. It is not an exhaustive census of R1 teaching, all theories, or all economics disciplines. Institution course boundaries vary; the same topic can occur at multiple levels. Graduate proof-based competence cannot be established by a binary numeric bank alone. Before publishing coverage claims, connect each listed concept to released, reviewed items and report uncovered concepts explicitly. Maintain source edition, learning objective, prerequisites, format, family identifier, and review status for every mapping.
