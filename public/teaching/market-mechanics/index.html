What the areas mean

Consumer surplus: How much better off buyers are because they pay less than the units are worth to them.

Producer surplus: How much better off sellers are because they receive more than the cost of providing those units. Fixed costs are not deducted here.

Total surplus: The value created by trade after subtracting production costs. With a policy, include government revenue or subtract government spending.

Deadweight loss: Value lost because beneficial trades do not happen, or because extra units cost more to produce than they are worth.

Price controls assume efficient rationing, lowest-cost production, no government purchases, and no waiting, enforcement, or search costs. Under inefficient rationing, welfare can be lower.
