Recent work focuses on the U.S. New Markets Tax Credit, where the Helfrich (2026) decomposition shows that the apparent rural mobilization gap is almost entirely between-CDE selection rather than a within-CDE rural penalty. The broader research program is the **Spatial Public-Finance Atlas**, a multi-program atlas of how public-subsidy capital flows across U.S. geography, with planned extensions to EU Cohesion Funds (with Katia Antunes) and to the African Development Bank's rural-finance portfolio (with Dr. Elizaveta Gonchar).

Currently writing two papers, drafting a visual textbook on statistical inference ([*Pictures of Inference*](https://github.com/ihelfrich/pictures-of-inference)), and tutoring economics students at the intermediate and graduate levels. I take new tutoring clients selectively. If you're a graduate student or early-career researcher working on applied econometrics, particularly in development or blended-finance contexts, [reach out](/contact/).
