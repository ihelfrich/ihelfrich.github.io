AI-exposure scores are often evaluated against outcomes that were available when the scores were constructed, or against measures built from the same conceptual ingredients. That can make a rating look validated even when the test is partly circular.

This sole-authored working paper asks a stricter question: when trained-human and GPT-4 ratings are compared with evidence that became available later, which task-level ratings continue to line up with observed task use?

The analysis links 19,265 occupation-task records across 923 occupations to six later public task-use measures. Within-occupation specifications hold constant broad occupational differences; paired cluster bootstraps compare the ratings on the same underlying records.

The paper is about measurement validity. It does not infer that an exposure score caused employment, wage, or task changes, and it does not convert predictive alignment into an employment-effect claim.
