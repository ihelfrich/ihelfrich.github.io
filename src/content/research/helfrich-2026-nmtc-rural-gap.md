abstract: "Decomposes the rural-versus-urban leverage gap in the U.S. New Markets Tax Credit using Community Development Entity fixed effects. The raw private-leverage gap of −0.262 falls to −0.047 (p = 0.64) after CDE identity enters the model. An order-invariant decomposition assigns −0.185 of the raw gap to intermediary composition, 88 percent of the explained movement. The decomposition moves the policy target upstream, from rural project markets to the CDFI Fund rules that determine which intermediaries receive allocation authority."

The paper makes three contributions. First, it constructs a project-level panel linking every NMTC project from FY2001 to FY2022 to the issuing CDE and the receiving QALICB, with rural/non-metro tract flagging from the OMB and ERS definitions. Second, it runs the leverage-gap decomposition with CDE fixed effects, showing that the apparent rural penalty lies between CDEs and not within them. Third, it tests for bunching at the 20% non-metro administrative target, which the proportionality instruction added to the code in 2006 governs and which is not itself statutory, using a Chetty-Kleven style discontinuity in the cumulative-deployment distribution. The null is clean: the mandate does not bind in cumulative-deployment data, suggesting CDEs satisfy the mandate ex post rather than treating it as a binding constraint ex ante. A subgroup scan across twenty-four cells leaves one exception standing after a multiple-comparison correction, rural commercial real-estate rehabilitation at −0.439, which the paper reports as exploratory because that dimension was added after an earlier draft flagged real estate.

The policy implication is that routing allocations toward high-leverage urban-experienced CDEs with rural carve-outs (or training rural-specialized CDEs to leverage more aggressively) is a much cheaper fix than subsidizing rural projects directly.
