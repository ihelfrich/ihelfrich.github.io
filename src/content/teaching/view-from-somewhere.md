The book has four parts. The first builds the idea: local views, gluing, the cycles and cuts of a network, least squares on a graph, and frames that need translation. The second applies it to price levels across countries, through index-number circularity, changing tastes, and countries that buy different goods. The third treats surveys that overlap, from statistical matching and the triangle inequalities to contextuality and question-order effects. The fourth covers inference where a statistic has a kink, and closes with a published psychophysical experiment re-examined.

Each chapter builds the idea, says what each paper claims and what it leaves unsaid, and reports the simulations run for it, corrections included. Its numbers come from seeded simulations and scripted calculations; the last chapter re-examines a published experiment. Twenty-two figures are live: the reader changes the inputs and the figure recomputes.
